# Design review, round 2 — Flourish Atlas after the 22 October fixes

This second round reviews the running app the day after the first review's fixes shipped (PRs #65 and #67, released as v0.10.0). It uses the same lay-visitor lens as `docs/reviews/design-2026-10-21.md` and the same rules (`docs/prompts/design-review-2026-10-21.md`). It does two things:

- it checks whether each of the first round's findings (F1–F22) is fixed;
- it reports what is new or still wrong (G1–G18).

Nothing in the app was changed.

**How it was reviewed.**
- **Code:** `main` at bcb951e, the v0.10.0 release, on real data. `data/static` was copied into `apps/web/public/data`, which held the synthetic fixtures before. The owner's `make api` (:8080) and `make web` (:5173) were already running.
- **Real-data check:** the Atlas lists 23 countries, with Indonesia first for the Secure Flourishing Index in 2023.
- **Screenshots:** every page at 320, 390, 768, 1280 and 1680 px, taken headlessly with Playwright (phones at 2×). Offline states were produced by blocking :8080 at the network level.
- **Measurements:** positions were measured from the DOM. Thin-cell counts come from the served static files.

**Screenshots** (outside git, in the session scratchpad): `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-nathann--claude-spares-spare-UaftH3/de90b4eb-36a7-4689-b6a4-50e1f47a70ee/scratchpad/shots/`. The scripts that made them (`capture.mjs`, `run.mjs`, `s1–s17.mjs`) sit one level up, and the downloaded CSV and PNG are in `dl/`.

**Not tested:**
- the loading skeleton;
- a real screen reader (the accessibility tree was read instead) and real touch hardware;
- the deployed site (it runs the same code and data as v0.10.0);
- what the picker shows when you open the empty "What matters to people" topic (the dev servers stopped before that check).

## Summary

1. **The first round's fixes worked on desktop.** At 1280 × 800 the Atlas now shows 12 of 23 countries before you scroll, where it showed 6; at 1680 it shows 22. Every page has the same grammar: question, then year and place, then a toolbar on the chart. One picker serves every page, and every chart sorts from its value label. Of the 22 findings:
   - 17 are fixed;
   - 3 are partly fixed;
   - 1 is fixed on desktop but worse on the phone;
   - 1 wasn't re-checked.

   See the table below.
2. **Segments puts countries in the wrong order (Blocker).** "Highest first" ranks each country by the plain average of its group means. One person in Egypt who chose "Prefer not to answer" for gender scored 9.00, and that puts Egypt first. Indonesia, the Atlas's top country, comes sixth. "Biggest gap first" has the same weakness (G1).
3. **Nothing marks an estimate that rests on very few people, except on Correlates (Blocker).** The served flag threshold is 0, so no row is ever flagged.
   - 1,602 of the 73,653 group means rest on **one person**, and 1,773 have an interval of zero width.
   - On the Atlas, "Taoist teachings important" (2023) puts Egypt, Germany, South Africa and Tanzania at the top with 10.0 [10.0, 10.0], one person each.
   - A zero-width interval looks like the *most* certain number on the page (G2).
4. **The phone got worse.** The chart's header now has seven parts, and the chart starts lower on every page than it did before the fixes:
   - Atlas: 636 → 757 px;
   - US States: ≈690 → 873 px, below the 844 px screen;
   - Correlates' strip: ≈830 → ≈930 px.

   The summary line and the subtitle say the same thing one under the other, and two rules sit 16 px apart (G3).
5. **The new chart toolbar crowds the chart title on desktop.** On Correlates a two-question title wraps to three lines at 28 px in a 350 px column. On US States the toolbar is five lines tall and leaves a 90 px gap under the title (G4).
6. **"Find a state" gives no feedback where you are looking.** Picking Ohio highlights row 45 of 50, below the screen, and the page doesn't move. On a phone the pinned card runs off the right edge ("on a 0–10 scal") (G5).
7. **Segments with two splits is unreadable.** The facets are 70 px wide on 0–10 axes, so every dot sits near 8 and the intervals vanish. Empty "Other" and "Prefer not to answer" rows repeat in every panel. On a phone the page is 18,845 px tall, about 22 screens (G6).
8. **The Codebook entry is now linked from every chart, and it is written for analysts.** The link reads "The exact question and answers". The page shows `scale_0_10`, "(DK)", "Non-response: yes", "n present" and countries in code order (G7).
9. **Downloads don't stand on their own.** The CSV names countries by number (1, 2, 3 …). The PNG has a title but no year, statistic, scale or key for the whiskers (G8).
10. **If you do three things:** fix the Segments order (G1, small); turn on the flag for thin estimates and give a one-person cell no interval (G2, small in code, an owner decision); and slim the phone's chart header (G3, small–medium).

## Findings

Severity: **Blocker** = misleads, or unusable at some width; **Major** = a lay visitor is slowed or confused; **Minor** = a careful eye notices. Kind: **usability** or **taste**. Screenshot names refer to the scratchpad folder above. Proposals that cross an owner decision or a project rule are flagged in bold.

### G1 · Segments orders countries by an average that one person can swing — Blocker · usability

- **Page:** Segments, `/segments?by=gender` (and every split), at every width.
- **Screenshots:** `seg-1280-gender.png`, `seg-1280-egypt-n1-hover.png`.
- **What's wrong:**
  - **The rule.** `facetOrder` (`apps/web/src/charts/SmallMultiples.tsx:84`) scores each country by the unweighted mean of its groups' estimates, and "Biggest gap first" by max − min.
  - **The cells.** Egypt's "Prefer not to answer" cell is one person, at 9.00 [9.00, 9.00]. That lifts Egypt's score to 7.88, so Egypt opens the page. Nigeria is second because its "Other" cell (one person) scored 8.17.
  - **The result.** Indonesia, whose men and women both score about 8.1, comes sixth.
  - **The rule it breaks.** The ordering is a statistic computed in the browser, which the project's rules reserve for the server.
- **Why it matters:** "Highest first" is a promise about the countries. Here the order comes from the smallest, noisiest cells, so a visitor reads Egypt as the most flourishing country by gender.
- **Change:**
  - Order panels by each country's own overall estimate for the same question, year and statistic. The Atlas's by-country file already serves it, and it is the server's number.
  - "Biggest gap first" needs a gap the server computes over groups above a size floor (or a decision to drop the option). **Changes what "Highest first" means on Segments; owner's call.**
  - Separately, whether "Prefer not to answer" belongs in a gender split is a catalog question (`meta.breakdown_labels.gender` lists it as level 4). It is out of scope for a design review, so it is flagged here only.
- **Effort:** small (overall order) + small–medium (server gap).

### G2 · Estimates resting on a handful of people carry no mark, and one person gets a zero-width interval — Blocker · usability

- **Page:** Atlas, Segments, US States, What Matters, Change: every page except Correlates.
- **Evidence:**
  - `seg-1280-egypt-n1-hover.png`: the card reads "Mean: 9.00 · 95% CI: [9.00, 9.00] · Based on 1 person".
  - The served `meta.suppression` is `{flag_below: 0, threshold: 0}`, so `flagged` is false on every row.
  - Counted from the static tier's mean files:

    | Rows | All | Under 30 people | One person | Zero-width interval |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | Group means (split cells) | 73,653 | 7,958 | 1,602 | 1,773 |
    | Country estimates | 2,127 | 120 | 34 | 39 |

  - Example: `/?outcome=TEACHINGS_10` (Taoist teachings important, 2023). Egypt, Germany, South Africa and Tanzania each show 10.0 [10.0, 10.0] from one person. Mexico, from two, shows [7.38, 11.50], an interval past the top of a 0–10 scale.
- **What's wrong:** `CLAUDE.md` and ADR-0019 say an estimate few people are behind "is shown with an asterisk". Only Correlates does it, with its own thresholds. Everywhere else a one-person dot is drawn as a dot with no whisker. That is the look a visitor has just learned, from the new key, to read as "very certain".
- **Why it matters:** the key and Methods teach "a short whisker means a sure number". These cells turn that lesson upside down.
- **Change:**
  1. **Turn on the flag.** Serve a `flag_below` (e.g. 30, or the old 50), keeping `threshold` at 0, so every cell is still shown (ADR-0011). Draw flagged dots hollow, add an asterisk after the value, and have the card say "Few people — treat with care". The key gains "* few people".
  2. **One person has no interval.** For n = 1 (or df < 1), the engine should serve no interval, and the card should say "no 95% CI: one person". **This changes what is served (a stats decision): note it and decide; it is outside a design review.**
  3. **Intervals past the scale.** An interval that runs past the scale's ends (Mexico's 11.50) is a separate stats question, and is noted only.
  - **Owner decisions:** the threshold, and whether ADR-0011's policy line changes.
- **Effort:** small (flag on, hollow dot, asterisk) + small (server interval rule).

### G9 · Correlates' captions name one question, and some names have no direction — Major · usability
