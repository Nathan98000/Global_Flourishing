# Design review — Flourish Atlas through a lay visitor's eyes, 21 October 2026

Brief: `docs/prompts/design-review-2026-10-21.md`. This review looks at the running app as someone curious about wellbeing would, with no statistics training. It asks three things: can they understand it, is it pleasant to look at, and can the controls at the top of each page be made friendlier. The third gets the most space. Nothing in the app was changed.

**How it was reviewed.** Branch `claude/charts-only-methods` (PR #65 as pushed, 868d793), on real data: `data/static` copied into `apps/web/public/data`, `make api` on :8080, `make web` on :5173. Checked: the Atlas lists 23 countries, with Indonesia first for the Secure Flourishing Index in 2023. I looked at every page in the browser pane at 390, 768, 1280 and 1680 px wide, then saved screenshots headlessly (Playwright, 800–1050 px tall screens, phones at 2×). The offline states were produced by blocking :8080 at the network level. The loading states were produced by delaying responses.

**Screenshots** (97 files, outside git) are in the session scratchpad: `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-nathann-Downloads-Projects-Global-Flourishing/164f5a97-21ad-4c72-9d13-cca349a53bc9/scratchpad/shots/`. The script and the page lists that made them sit one level up (`capture.mjs`, `spec1–5.json`), so they can be made again. Every finding gives its file name.

**Not tested:** CSV/PNG downloads, a real screen reader (accessible names were read from the accessibility tree), real touch hardware.

**Mockups:** [`design-2026-10-21-controls.html`](design-2026-10-21-controls.html). Open it from this folder: it uses the app's own font files through a relative path.

## Summary

1. **On the US States map, the darkest states are the least certain ones (Blocker).** Delaware, Rhode Island, Alaska and South Dakota rest on 102–148 people each and come out darkest. Ohio rests on 1,495 people, and its interval overlaps the US figure, yet it comes out among the lightest. Someone checking their own state comes away with the wrong idea. Make the dot chart, which shows intervals, the default view. Add "Find a state", and mark the thin estimates (F1).
2. **The What Matters grid misleads anyone reading across a row (Blocker).** Each column is shaded on its own range. So in Brazil's row, "Being happy" (9.15) looks darker than "Being healthy" (9.31), and "Religious or spiritual life" (8.62) looks darkest of all. Reading across a row is exactly how you answer "what matters most in Brazil?". Shade the whole grid on one range (F2).
3. **The controls take most of the first screen.** At 1280 × 800 the Atlas shows 6 of its 23 countries before you scroll. On a phone the first country appears at 636 px of 844. Opening "More options" on a phone adds about 430 px more controls above the chart (F3).
4. **Choosing what to look at takes three controls: Topic, Measure and "Or search".** The topic you need is a guess: life satisfaction sits under "Flourishing index questions", not "Wellbeing". On a phone, Topic is folded *below* the Measure list it filters (F4).
5. **The recommended reorganisation is option B, "question bar + chart toolbar".** One question button replaces the three pickers: it is the Correlates picker, with search and browse-by-topic in one panel. Year and Countries sit beside it, in the same order on every page. Everything about how the chart is drawn (average or middle value, map or chart, correlation type) moves into the chart's own header. Sorting moves onto the chart's column labels, as What Matters already works. That is one row of controls instead of two to four; on a phone it is two controls and one Options button. Effort is medium, with about 5 kB more on the first page load (estimated).
6. **Sort and Order are two controls for one idea, and they drift apart.** On Change at 1280, Sort ends the first row and Order starts the second (F5).
7. **A greyed-out choice explains itself only on mouse hover.** The disabled "Midyear" carries its reason in a `title`, which touch screens never show and screen readers aren't pointed to. The component already supports a visible note (F6).
8. **Correlates opens on the United States while every other page opens on all countries**, and it has two separate controls for place. Asked "do churchgoers find more meaning?", a visitor reads the US figure (+0.33) as the answer for the world (the average across countries is +0.15) (F7).
9. **Tablet visitors can't see half the site.** At 768 px the nav folds US States, Segments and Methods into "More", although all seven links would fit on the row (F8).
10. **What works should stay:** the question picker on Correlates, the "How to read the charts" diagram on Methods, the hover card's four lines, the warm paper-and-teal look, header sorting on What Matters, the Change chart's zero line with "a rise is better", and the empty states that say what to do. These are listed under **Keep** below.

### Evaluation

**Choosing what to look at.** Topic, Measure and Or search are three ways to pick one thing, and each falls short alone. Topic names are owner-chosen display names (ADR-0016), but which measure sits under which topic is the catalog's, and a lay visitor can't predict it (life satisfaction is a "Flourishing index question"). The Measure select holds only one topic's items, so browsing across topics is a loop: change Topic, look, change again. Search is the fastest route but is labelled "Or search", as if it were the fallback. On a phone it is folded away.

The Correlates **question picker** already solves all three in one panel: a search field, topics on the left, each topic's questions on the right with a line of wording, recently used questions, and unavailable questions left in view with the reason in words (`t3-1280-picker-open.png`). Its own header comment says "the Atlas and US States may adopt it later". It should be the one way to choose a question on every page.

Could every page lead with a sentence like Correlates' "How do answers to … relate to …?" It works there because the page *is* a two-noun question. Elsewhere it strains: answer levels ("More than once a week"), sort orders and the correlation type are not nouns in a question. Long names wrap the sentence over three lines at 1280. That is option A below.

**Grouping and order.** There is no shared grammar today. The *what* (Topic, Measure, search) always leads. After that, *when and where* (Wave, Countries) and *how it's drawn* (Statistic, Sort, Order, View, Answer level) are interleaved, and their order is set by flex-wrap, not by meaning. Countries lands alone on a third row at 1280 and 1680 because the four segmented groups before it fill the row (`atlas-1280-top.png`, `atlas-1680-top.png`). On categorical questions, where Statistic has two options instead of three, it fits on the second row, so the layout jumps as you change questions. Segments puts "Second breakdown" beside its select while every other label sits above. The controls that change *what is measured* (question, year, countries, splits) deserve the top. The ones that change *how it is drawn* belong with the chart they affect, next to Download.

**Sorting.** Sort (By value · A–Z) and Order (High to low · Low to high) are one idea in two controls. They sit apart at 768 on the Atlas, and at 1280 on Change, on Change with a categorical question, and on What Matters' Other questions (`change-1280-top.png`, `atlas-768-top.png`, `change-1280-categorical.png`, `wm-1280-questions.png`). What Matters now sorts by clicking a column label, with a second click reversing it (ADR-0026). The dot plots and bar charts can do the same with two labels over the chart: the country column ("Country", A–Z) and the value column ("Average", high first). One rule on every chart; `sort` and `dir` stay the URL state. On a phone, one "Order" choice (Highest first · Lowest first · A–Z) in the Options sheet does the same job.

**Words.** See the glossary. The control words that matter most: Wave → Year; Statistic → Show; Mean → Average; Median → Middle value (hint: "half answered above, half below"); Distribution → All answers; Answer level → "Showing % who said"; Break down by / Second breakdown → Split by … then by …; By gap → Biggest gap first; Correlation type: Straight-line / By rank → "Measured as: straight-line / by rank", with the existing "What's the difference?" link; Adjusted state weights → "Adjust for who answered in each state", with a one-line hint. Two words need a hint rather than a new name: **Midyear** ("a shorter survey between the two years, Nov 2023–Dec 2024") and **Correlation** ("how closely two answers rise and fall together, from −1 to +1").

**Unavailable choices.** Only US States' checkbox says why in plain view. The greyed "Midyear" on the Atlas, Change, Segments and US States says why only in a hover `title`. On a phone that is invisible; to a screen reader it isn't pointed to (no `aria-describedby`); and it looks merely pale, not unavailable (`atlas-1280-top.png`). `RadioRow` already has a `note` prop for this.

**State at a glance.** After a few changes, the only summary is the chart subtitle ("Average score, 0–10 · Wave 1, 2023"). It leaves out the countries shown and the order, and uses "Wave 1" where the control said "2023". Making the subtitle carry the state in the controls' own words ("Average score, 0–10 · 2023 · all countries · highest first") answers "what am I looking at?" without a new element. On a phone, the same words become the summary line next to the Options button, which is Correlates' phone pattern applied everywhere.

**Phone.** Folding is right; what is folded is wrong. The fold holds the *topic* (part of choosing the question) and keeps the *sort* (a chart concern) in the same list. When opened it adds about 430 px of stacked controls between the question and the chart, and its button keeps saying "More options" with no sign it is open (`t1-390-more-open.png`). The phone should show the question button and the year, then the chart. Everything about how it's drawn goes into a sheet that rises over the chart (option B's mockup).

**Weight on the page.** At 1280 the deck and controls fill the top 410 px of an 800 px screen; with the chart's header, the first data mark is at 647. The segmented groups are quiet, as ADR-0012 intended. But there are five to eight labelled boxes, each with a label line, so the block reads as a form you must fill in before you are allowed a chart.

### Three reorganisations

The mockups show the Atlas, Correlates and Segments at 1280 px (the app's 896 px column) and the Atlas at 390 px for each option, plus "Today" for comparison. The picker, the phone sheet and header sorting are clickable in option B.

#### A · Ask it as a sentence

Every page leads with its question as a sentence. Each noun is a button: the question opens the picker; the year and the countries open short lists.

- Atlas: "How do people rate [life satisfaction ▾] in [2023 ▾] across [all countries ▾]?"
- Segments: "How does [life satisfaction ▾] differ by [age ▾] [+ and by …] in [2023 ▾] across [all countries ▾]?"
- Correlates keeps its sentence and adds the year and the place.

The settings that aren't nouns (show, order, correlation type) become two quiet words in the chart header.

- **Other pages:** Change: "How did [life satisfaction] change from 2023 to 2024 for the same people in [all countries]?" What Matters: "What matters most to people in [Brazil], [by age]?" US States: "How do people rate [life satisfaction] in each state, [2023]?"
- **Fixes:** the controls read as the visitor's own question; the sentence is the summary; three pickers become one.
- **Costs:** every page needs its own grammar. Long names ("Feelings about household income") wrap the sentence over three lines at 1280 and five at 390. Answer levels don't fit a slot. A returning visitor can't scan a sentence for the one setting they want. The rest of the settings still need a second home. **Large.**

#### B · Question bar on top, toolbar on the chart (recommended)

The top of every page holds what you're looking at, then when and where, in that order:

- **Question** — one button showing the question's name and a line of its wording. It opens the Correlates picker: search and browse by topic in one panel, a full-screen sheet on a phone.
- **Year** — the segmented group, with any unavailable year's reason in a visible line under it.
- **Countries** — the existing popover, styled as a button with ▾ rather than a text box.

Everything about how the chart is drawn moves into the **chart header's right side**, where Download CSV · PNG sits today: Show (Average · Middle value · All answers), Map/Chart, Measured as, Order countries (Segments), plus "How to read this chart" and Download. **Sorting** moves onto the chart's column labels ("Country ⇅", "Average ↓"), one click to sort and again to reverse, as ADR-0026 does for What Matters. The subtitle carries the state ("Average score, 0–10 · 2023 · all countries · highest first").

- **Atlas, 1280:** one row of controls (Question · Year · Countries), about 70 px instead of 193.
- **Correlates:** the sentence *is* its question bar. One "Where" select (All countries, averaged · Each country side by side · then each country) replaces Country plus "In United States | Country by country". "Measured as" moves to the chart header.
- **Segments:** Question · "Split by [Age] then [nothing]" · Year · Countries; "Order countries" goes in the chart header.
- **Change:** Question · Countries (its years are fixed); the answer level for categorical questions goes in the chart header.
- **What Matters:** the three views stay as tabs. Within a country gets Country · Split by on top; Other questions gets Question on top and Answer in the header. Sorting is already by labels.
- **US States:** Question · Year on top. In the header: Map/Chart, Show, the adjusted-weights switch with its reason, and a new **Find a state** select that pins that state's card (F1).
- **Phone:** Question button and Year, then a summary line ("Average · all countries · highest first") with an **Options** button. Options opens a sheet with Show, Order and Countries. Header labels stay tappable for sorting.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 + scrolling back | 2 (question, pick) |
| 2 | 5 | 4 |
| 5 | 4–5 | 3, with Find a state |

- **Fixes:** F3, F4, F5, F6 (by moving the reason into the Year row), F17 (one place for downloads), the Countries control that looks like a text field, and part of F7 (one "Where").
- **Costs:**
  - The question picker moves from the Correlates chunk into the first page load. Its source is 7.6 kB + 2.7 kB gzipped; compiled, it adds about 5 kB (estimate), taking 206 → about 211 of 250.
  - Sorting by label has to look clickable: an arrow on every sortable label, a focus ring, a 44 px target on phones.
  - Header controls must stay reachable by keyboard and named for screen readers, in a sensible tab order (top bar, then chart header, then chart).
  - It **revises an owner decision**: the two-step Topic → Measure picker ("owner decision 1" in `OutcomePicker.tsx`; ADR-0016 for the subtopic step). Browsing by topic survives inside the panel, and the `topic` URL parameter still opens the picker on that topic.
  - It **extends ADR-0026** (sorting from labels) to every chart.
  - **Medium**: one shared component already exists for the picker; the chart header toolbar and label sorting are new shared pieces; each view's controls are re-plumbed without touching URL state.

#### C · A settings panel beside the chart

On a desktop, every control moves into a 260 px column at the left, under three headings: Question, Year & place, Display. The chart takes the rest. On a tablet or a phone the column becomes a summary line over the chart with **Edit**, which opens the same form as a sheet.

- **Other pages:** the same three groups everywhere; Correlates' and What Matters' tabs stay above the panel.
- **Fixes:** the chart is the largest thing on the first screen; one form, one order; a summary line.
- **Costs:** either every chart loses about 290 px at 1280 (896 → about 600, cramping Segments' facets and the What Matters grid), or the page grows past its 896 px reading column (drawn at 1184 px) and stops lining up with Methods. The look turns from publication to dashboard, against ADR-0012's "data publication" intent. On a phone every change is two taps. **Large.**

### Recommendation

**Option B.** It is the only option that gives every page the same grammar (what, then when and where, then how it's drawn) while keeping each control next to the thing it changes. It reuses the best control the app already has (the Correlates picker) and the sorting rule the owner has already chosen for What Matters. It brings the chart onto the first screen at every width, without turning the publication into a dashboard (C) or forcing every page into a sentence (A). Borrow A's best idea inside it: the chart subtitle, and the phone's summary line, carry the current state in plain words.

What option B must not break (see "Rules" in the draft prompt below):

- The URL parameters keep their names and values, and old links keep working.
- Control words are the app's own and may change; question names and answers still come from the server.
- Every number keeps its card with the 95% CI and n.

## Clarity and look, in brief

**The deck under the nav.** Each page's deck has its own voice and length:

- the Atlas: four lines, with the sample count in ink;
- Correlates: one plain line;
- What Matters: five lines listing all seven items;
- US States: opens on "the survey's state weights";
- Segments: "the Atlas's estimates, cut finer".

One pattern would serve every page: sentence one says what you can see in everyday words, sentence two says what to do first. Suggested rewrites are in F18.

**Chart titles and subtitles.** Titles carry the measure, which is good. Subtitles mix the controls' words with the data's: "Wave 1, 2023" where the control says 2023; "Average score" where the card says "Mean"; "×" in Segments titles; "pp" on Change. The direction of better is stated on Change ("a rise is better") but nowhere else. The question wording, the most useful line for a lay reader, is set in the same 13 px grey as the units line.

**How sure the numbers are.** The whiskers, the asterisks and the card's "95% CI: [X, Y] · Based on N people" are all present and consistent (ADR-0022/0024), and the card's "Based on 1,495 people" is exactly right for a lay reader. What's missing is a way to learn what they mean *where the question arises*. The Methods page has an excellent annotated diagram ("How to read the charts"), but no chart links to it, and no chart has a key for the whisker (F10).

**Typography.** The type scale (12/13/15/16.5/19/21/22/28) is consistent, and the serif–sans split works. The deck runs about 60–65 characters a line, and Methods prose 16.5 px at about 600 px, both comfortable. Numbers are tabular. Two weaknesses:

- **Too many lines at 13 px grey.** Subtitle, wording, notes and the US figures all sit at that size and colour, so the chart header has no second level.
- **Long titles on a phone.** Phone chart titles wrap to three lines at 28 px on Correlates ("Life evaluation today and Feelings about household income", `correlates-390-top.png`).

**Spacing and alignment.** Control rows are evenly spaced (16 px gaps). But the gap from the wording line to the chart's axis is about 60 px at 1280, and the gap from the controls' rule to the title is 55 px, so the chart floats away from its own title (`atlas-1280-top.png`). The What Matters grid stops 82 px short of the column's right edge (`t4-1280-matrix-full.png`). At 1680 the layout is identical to 1280 (a fixed 896 px column, `atlas-1680-top.png`). That is right for reading, but it means the Countries orphan row appears at every desktop width.

**Colour.** The warm paper, ink and teal identity is used consistently:

- Teal for marks and links.
- Ink rules (not teal fills) for selected segments.
- Sand→teal for the grid and the map.

The accent does neither too much nor too little. Two exceptions:

- Correlates' Compare two draws its answer bars in warm grey, the only grey data marks in the app.
- The map and the What Matters grid use the ramp to rank things the intervals can't tell apart (F1, F2).

**Consistency.** Segmented groups, selects and links are drawn the same everywhere. Four things are not:

- **The Countries control** looks like an empty text field (white box, "All 23", no ▾) rather than a button.
- **Download CSV · PNG** sits right of the title on most pages but under the subtitle on Correlates and on every phone page.
- **Link text** varies: "See all twelve questions →", "Answer codes & details →" and "What's the difference?" are three styles for one idea.

## Findings

Severity: **Blocker** = misleads, or unusable at some width; **Major** = a lay visitor is slowed or confused; **Minor** = a careful eye notices. Kind: **usability** (slowed, confused or misled) or **taste** (it would look better). Screenshot names are in the scratchpad folder given at the top. Proposals that cross an owner decision or a project rule are flagged in bold.

### F1 · The US map's darkest states are the least certain ones — Blocker · usability

- **Page:** US States, `/states` (also `?view=bars`), at 1280 and 390.
- **Screenshots:** `t5-1280-map-ohio.png`, `states-1280-full.png`, `t5-390-map-full.png`, `t5-1280-chart-full.png`.
- **What's wrong:**
  - The colour window runs from 6.99 to 7.61 on a 0–10 scale.
  - The four highest-scoring, darkest states rest on 148, 107, 103 and 102 people: DE 7.61 [7.06, 8.15], RI, AK, SD.
  - Ohio (1,495 people, 7.12 [6.98, 7.25]) is one of the palest, though its interval overlaps the US figure (7.22 [7.20, 7.25]).
  - The map shows no intervals and no state names, and the thin estimates carry no asterisk (`flagged` is false at n ≈ 100 under ADR-0011's defaults).
- **Why it matters:** "How does my state compare?" is the page's whole promise. The colours answer it with the noisiest numbers in the release, so a visitor from Ohio leaves thinking their state is near the bottom.
- **Change:**
  1. Open the page on the **Chart** view (dot plot with intervals and the US line) and keep **Map** one click away. This changes `view`'s default, so old links without `view` would open the chart: **changes a URL default — say so in an ADR**, or keep the default and send the nav link to `?view=bars`.
  2. Add **Find a state** to the chart header: a select of `meta.state_labels` that highlights the state on map and chart and pins its card.
  3. Mark thin estimates on the map (hatched or outlined, as Compare two's asterisked cells are). The server would decide which rows are thin (a `flagged` rule for states), because the front end computes no statistics.
  4. Optional: one line in the card, "US overall: 7.22 [7.20, 7.25]". **This changes the card format of ADR-0022**; it's worth it on this page only.
- **Effort:** medium.

### F2 · The What Matters grid shades each column on its own range — Blocker · usability

- **Page:** What Matters, `/what-matters`, at 1280; also on the phone.
- **Screenshots:** `t4-1280-matrix-full.png`, `whatmatters-390-full.png`.
- **What's wrong:**
  - Within each column the darkest cell is that column's highest. Across a row, shades mean nothing.
  - Brazil: "Being happy" 9.15 is darker than "Being healthy" 9.31.
  - Brazil: "Religious or spiritual life" 8.62 is as dark as the row's 9.49.
  - Sweden's "Religious or spiritual life" 2.76 and Japan's "Being healthy" 7.99 sit in neighbouring pale shades.
  - The only warning is the small "each column shaded on its own range" beside the key.
- **Why it matters:** the page's question is "what matters most?", which people answer by reading across their own country's row. The colours give the wrong order.
- **Change:** shade the whole grid on one window fitted to the data (ADR-0010's rule), with one key. If column contrast is still wanted, use it only while a column is the sort key. **This reverses the per-column shading accepted with the What Matters PR (#53, not recorded in an ADR)**; record the new rule in ADR-0026 or a new ADR.
- **Effort:** small.

# Controls — a question bar on top, a toolbar on the chart

From the design review of 21 October 2026 (`docs/reviews/design-2026-10-21.md`,
"Controls", option B; mockups in `docs/reviews/design-2026-10-21-controls.html`).
The owner chose option B on <date>. This prompt reorganises the controls at the
top of the Atlas, Change, What Matters, Correlates, US States and Segments. It
changes no statistics, no API and no URL parameter.

## How to work

- Branch `claude/controls-question-bar` from `main`. One conventional commit per
  task (`feat(web): …`, `docs(adr): …`); the first commit includes this prompt.
- Open a PR at the end with one line per task. Don't merge, tag or deploy.
- Run the dev loop on real data (`rsync -a --delete --exclude .DS_Store
  data/static/ apps/web/public/data/`, `make api`, `make web`). If anything runs
  `make web-fixtures`, rsync again before checking by eye. Leave port 4173 alone.
- **Rules (each is a project rule or an owner decision):**
  - The URL is the state: `outcome`, `topic`, `wave`, `stat`, `sort`, `dir`,
    `countries`, `level`, `by`, `view`, `country`, `scope`, `method`, `other`,
    `a`, `b`, `vars`, `order`, `qsort`, `qdir`, `adj` keep their names, values
    and defaults. Every old link opens the same view as before. Add a
    `search.test.ts` case per page proving it.
  - The front end owns no labels: question names, answers, country and state
    names come from the server. Only control words (legends, option names,
    hints) are the app's and change here.
  - Every number keeps its hover card with `95% CI: [X, Y]` and the n
    (ADR-0022, ADR-0024). No data tables, no line under a chart, one light
    theme, no world map, Codebook through Methods, every state on its own.
  - Accessibility: every control reachable by keyboard and named; 44 px
    targets under 40rem; new colour pairs added to `tokens.test.ts`.
  - First load ≤ 250 kB gzipped (`pnpm -C apps/web build && pnpm -C apps/web
    budget`); report the before and after. No new runtime dependency.
  - The Atlas, Segments and What Matters keep working with :8080 blocked.

## Tasks

### 1 · Plain words for the controls

- Wave → Year; Statistic → Show; Mean → Average; Median → Middle value;
  Distribution → All answers; Share → "% who said"; Answer level → "Showing %
  who said"; Break down by → Split by; Second breakdown → then by; By gap →
  Biggest gap first; Correlation type → Measured as; Adjusted state weights →
  Adjust for who answered in each state.
- `WAVE_TITLES` drops "Wave 1/2": subtitles read "2023", "2024" and "Midyear
  survey, Nov 2023–Dec 2024".
- Units: "pp" → "percentage points"; "×" in titles → "by".
- The hover card's own words don't change (ADR-0022).
- **Tests:** views, components and e2e selectors that name these labels.

### 2 · Unavailable choices say why, in view

- Every disabled option has a visible reason line, using `RadioRow`'s `note` or
  `noteId`, and is drawn as unavailable (dashed outline), not just paler.
- Offline: Median is disabled with "needs the live data service"; a
  `stat=median` link offline says so instead of "invalid URL parameters".
- **Tests:** the note's text and `aria-describedby` on Atlas, Change, Segments
  and US States; the offline median notice (journey 6).

### 3 · One question picker

- `QuestionPicker` (single-select) replaces `OutcomePicker` on the Atlas,
  Change, US States, Segments and What Matters' Other questions.
- Closed, it is one button: the question's short name over a line of its
  wording, and "Change ▾".
- It is limited to each page's servable questions, with unavailable ones
  listed with their reason, as on Correlates.
- A `topic` in the URL opens the picker on that topic. Picking a question
  writes `outcome` (and clears `level`) exactly as the old picker did.
- Under 600 px it is a full-screen sheet (as now).
- **ADR:** a new ADR records that this revises the two-step picker (owner
  decision 1 in `OutcomePicker.tsx`) and ADR-0016's subtopic step, both kept as
  the panel's browse column. Remove `OutcomePicker` once nothing uses it.
- **Budget:** the picker moves into the initial chunk; record the gzipped
  delta in the ADR.
- **Tests:** `picker.test.tsx` → `questionPicker.test.tsx` cases per page;
  journey 1 (change topic, measure and wave via the picker, share, reload).

### 4 · The question bar: same order on every page

- Top row, in this order: Question · Year · Countries.
- Segments adds "Split by [ ] then [ ]" after Question.
- Change has no Year (its years are fixed).
- Correlates keeps its sentence as its question bar, then Year · **Where**.
  Where is one select ("All countries, averaged" · "Each country side by
  side" · each country) that writes the existing `country` and `scope`
  parameters.
- The Countries control becomes a button ("All countries ▾"), with an "All
  countries" choice at the top of its panel.
- What Matters keeps its three views as tabs; Within a country's top row is
  Country · Split by.
- **Tests:** a layout test that the three controls render in order on each
  page; Correlates' Where writes the same URLs as today's two controls
  (journey 10).

### 5 · The chart toolbar

- `ChartFigure` gets a `toolbar` slot on the header's right, replacing the
  "Download CSV · PNG" line: page controls, then "How to read this chart"
  (→ `/methods#how-to-read-the-charts`), then one "Download ▾" (CSV, PNG).
- What goes in it: Show (Atlas, US States); Map/Chart and the adjustment
  switch with its reason (US States); Measured as and "What's the
  difference?" (Correlates); Order countries (Segments); the answer
  (Change, What Matters' Other questions, Atlas categorical).
- The subtitle carries the state in the controls' words: "Average score,
  0–10 · 2023 · all countries · highest first".
- **Tests:** export journey 4 (CSV) through the new menu; subtitle text.

### 6 · Sort from the chart's labels

- Dot plots and bar charts (Atlas, Change, US States chart, What Matters'
  Other questions) get two header labels over the chart: the names column
  ("Country" / "State") and the value column (the statistic's own word).
- Each label is a button with `aria-sort` and an arrow; one click sorts
  (names A–Z, values high first), a second click reverses. They write `sort`
  and `dir` as the old controls did.
- The Sort and Order controls go. Segments' country order stays a toolbar
  select (its panels have no value column).
- **ADR:** extend ADR-0026 (or the task-3 ADR) to every chart.
- **Tests:** `sortRows.test.ts` unchanged; per-view tests click the labels;
  the URL matches the old controls' URLs for each state.

### 7 · The phone

- Under 40rem: Question and Year on top, then a summary line ("Average · all
  countries · highest first") with an **Options** button that opens a sheet
  (Show, Order, Countries, and the page's toolbar controls).
- The "More options" `<details>` goes.
- Header labels stay tappable for sorting.
- The sheet is a dialog: focus moves in, Escape closes, focus returns.
- **Tests:** journeys 11 and 12 (no sideways scroll, 320–390 px, both font
  sizes) with the sheet open; a new journey: phone, choose a question,
  change the order in Options, reload.

### 8 · Docs

- `docs/METHODS.md` "How to read the charts" mentions the header sort and the
  toolbar.
- Update the README screenshots if any show controls.
