# UX, visual-design and data-visualization QA — Flourish Atlas, 24 September 2026

Reviewer pass over the running dev build (`http://localhost:5173`, API on `:8080`), after the Phase 5 (Change, Compare, What Matters, US States) and Phase 6 (Correlates, Model cards) merges. Every finding below was observed in the browser and re-checked before it went into this file. Where a finding depends on numbers, they were read from the page's own data tables, tooltips, or the static tier (`/data/variables.json`), not from source.

**How it was tested.** Chrome, light and dark themes. Viewports: 1280 px desktop (primary), 768 px (tablet portrait), 735 px (half-screen laptop), 640 px, 390 px (phone). Every route in the nav plus `/model-cards`, `/codebook/$name` and a 404. Controls, pickers, sort toggles, tooltips, the country popovers, search, back/forward and URL state were exercised. **Not tested:** CSV/PNG downloads (browser download permission), screen-reader output, keyboard-only journeys beyond focus rings.

**Legend.** Severity: **Critical / High / Medium / Low**. Type: **Bug** = objectively wrong (overlap, clipping, wrong copy, wrong number, broken control). **Design** = a recommendation; reasonable people could choose otherwise.

---

## A. Overall assessment

The visual system is coherent and restrained. The warm paper palette, serif headings, quiet segmented controls, value-label column and footnote pattern read as one product on the pages built during the redesign (Atlas, Change's main chart, the Atlas bar chart for categorical items). The best charts on the site are excellent: a first-time visitor can read them without help.

The newest pages are a different story. They reuse the same components but lose the discipline those components had. Three kinds of problems recur:

1. **Numbers that read backwards or mean something other than they appear to.** Change averages raw answer codes, so "+0.09" on a Yes(1)/No(2) item means *fewer* people said Yes. Correlates ranks items with n = 2–7 at the top of every country's list, with r = ±1.00. The Atlas "Distribution" of the flourishing index drops about 90% of respondents. None of this looks broken. It looks authoritative, and that makes it the most urgent class of problem.
2. **Faceted charts that break when the facet count grows.** Compare (split), Breakdowns (second breakdown) and What Matters produce very tall grids whose column labels sit only at the bottom, overlap each other, or are missing from tooltips. Axis windows run past the scale's end (2–12 and 0–15 on a 0–10 scale).
3. **Implementation details that surface as UI.** Weight codes (`w_c1`, `w_l2`, `w_state_c1`) are in every tooltip. State groups are labelled `ND_SD_WY`. There are three controls that can never be enabled and a section whose only content is "not possible from this release's data". A server error is reported as "Live data service is offline".

None of this needs a redesign. Most fixes are local: orient signs using the catalog's existing `direction` field, set a minimum n for rankings, put facet labels at the top, clamp domains to the scale, and clean up copy.

## B. New-page review

### Change (`/change`) — Phase 5

What works: the main dot plot is the cleanest chart on the new pages. It has a zero line, signed value labels and CIs, and "a rise is better" in the subtitle when the catalog knows a direction. The "Asked only once" empty state is specific and actionable. The transition table ("each row sums to 100%", "First answer ↓ · later answer →") is a model of self-explaining design.

Most important issues:

- **C1 (Critical)** Signs and units are raw answer codes. Binary and reverse-coded ordinal items read backwards. See §C.
- **H6 (High)** Three of the four "Compare" options can never be enabled. The measure picker offers 71 measures that cannot show change.
- **M6 (Medium)** The individual-change charts only appear after picking ≤ 4 countries. The only prompt is a line of text *below* the chart that refers to "above".
- **M8 (Medium)** With 1–2 countries selected, the dot plot collapses: ticks jam into the row and the zero line becomes a stub. For SFI, no individual-change chart appears at all, and nothing explains why.
- **Data to verify:** Egypt, religious service attendance. 52.8% of those who said "Once a week" in 2023 said "Never" in 2024, and Egypt's mean shift (+0.54) is 4.5× the next country's. This may be real, or it may be a coding or mode change in Egypt's Wave 2. Either way it currently headlines the view.

### Compare (`/compare`) — Phase 5

What works: the concept (domains side by side for 2–5 countries), the "Remove …" link for the added measure, and the tooltip content.

Most important issues:

- **The page opens empty.** "Choose two to five countries" is shown, and the picker button reads "Choose up to 5 countries". After picking, it reads "4 of 5 countries", which suggests 5 countries already exist (M6).
- **C5 (Critical)** "Split each country by" makes the chart unreadable. Country names appear only at the very bottom of a ~1,500 px figure. Facet titles overlap ("SFI: happiness & life satisfacSFI: happiness…"). Tick labels collide ("10.02.5"). Tooltips omit the country.
- **M7 (Medium)** Each domain gets its own hue (orange, green, purple, blue, pink, red-orange). The colour repeats the facet title and encodes nothing. The added-measure panel then reverts to the house green. There are no value labels, unlike Atlas and Change. The bottom axis labels of every facet sit on the same line as the last row ("Japan 3", with Japan's dot touching the "6" tick).

### What Matters (`/what-matters`) — Phase 5

What works: "The other midyear questions" section is a strong chart: a % axis, value labels, and an "Answer level" control reused from Atlas.

Most important issues:

- **H1 (High)** The x-axis runs **2 to 12** on a 0–10 importance scale (0–15 on a phone).
- **H7 (High)** The page is one ~5,000 px column of 23 small multiples, and the other three sections sit below it. "What matters most" never actually ranks the items: the order is fixed questionnaire order. "Two things that travel together" contains nothing except two paragraphs explaining it is "not possible from this release's data".
- **H11 (High, phone)** Row labels are clipped at the left edge ("e: being healthy", "s or spiritual life").
- The "By value" sort is not defined: by value of which of the seven items? Section headings (h3) are visually smaller than the chart titles beneath them (M15).

### US States (`/states`) — Phase 5

What works: the pooled-state approach, the "US overall on the national weight" reference line, the map/chart toggle, and the Hover-for-CI footnote on the map.

Most important issues:

- **C6 (Critical, pending re-verification)** "2024" and "Midyear" both show "Live data service is offline" even though the API is up. `/v1/states?wave=Y2` and `?wave=MY` return a response without CORS headers, while `/health` returns 200 and Wave 1 works.
- **H9 (High)** 37 of the 41 plotted state and pool estimates sit *above* the "US overall" line, including CA, TX, NY, FL, PA, IL and OH. The national figure (national weight) and the state figures (state weights) are not on a comparable basis, and nothing says so.
- **H3 (High)** The chart view labels rows with USPS codes and raw pool keys (`LA`, `ND_SD_WY`, `AK_HI_MT`, `ME_NH_RI_VT`, `DE_MS_WV`). The tooltip reads "Wyoming (pooled as ND_SD_WY)".
- **H2 (High)** The lowest map bins are nearly indistinguishable from "no estimate". The ramp spans 0.5 points (6.99–7.5) while typical state CIs are ±0.2–0.3, so colour differences mostly show noise.
- The "Adjusted state weights" checkbox is disabled with a jargon explanation. Because of M5 it renders as a solid dark square that looks *checked*.

### Correlates (`/correlates`) and Model cards (`/model-cards`) — Phase 6

What works: "associations, not causes" appears in the lede and every footnote. The diverging rust/teal encoding is sound. The adjusted model has real CIs and a model card that states its control set.

Most important issues:

- **C2 (Critical)** The #1 "strongest association" in every country checked is a religion-teachings item with n = 2–7: Argentina "Islamic teachings important" +0.91 (n = 7), Germany "Bahá'í teachings important" −1.00 (n = 2), Japan +1.00 (n = 2), United States "Confucian teachings important" +0.92 (n = 6).
- **C3 (Critical)** Signs follow code order, not label meaning. "Feeling down or depressed **+0.44**" appears one row above "PHQ-2 depression score **−0.44**". "Feeling capable −0.37", "Life in balance −0.38" and "TIPI: calm, emotionally stable −0.36" all read as "the more capable/balanced/calm, the less flourishing".
- **H4 (High)** The "Across countries" matrix shows 9 of 23 countries at desktop with no scroll affordance. The last visible column is cut mid-word ("Japa"). On a phone the whole page scrolls sideways: the document is 2,087 px wide at 390 px.
- **H5 (High)** "Adjusted" silently changes the unit from r to *SFI points per SD*. Values exceed 1 (Australia +1.09, Japan +1.4), but the axis still reads −1.0…1.0 and the toggle label suggests an "adjusted correlation".
- Model cards is not in the nav, has no link back to Correlates, and uses a third page-header style. Its copy is developer-facing ("The API never chooses a weight itself").

## C. Visualization review — most important problems

### C1 · Change shows averages of raw answer codes — signs read backwards · **Critical · Bug**

**Location.** `/change`, main chart, "How individual answers moved" histogram, and data table. Reproduce with `?outcome=CLOSE_TO` and `?outcome=ATTEND_SVCS`.

**Problem.**

- *Has someone to confide in* is coded 1 = Yes, 2 = No. The subtitle reads "Average change on the 1–2 scale", and Poland shows **+0.09**. That means 9 percentage points *more* people answered **No**: fewer people have a confidant.
- *Religious service attendance* is coded 1 = More than once a week … 5 = Never. Egypt shows **+0.54**, which means people attended *less* often.
- The histogram's axis "Change in the answer (later minus earlier)", −4…+4, has the same inversion: +4 means "More than once a week → Never".

**Why it matters.** A first-time reader will take "+" as "more of the thing named in the title". For every binary item and every reverse-coded ordinal item, they will draw the opposite conclusion. "Average change on the 1–2 scale" is also not a unit anyone can interpret.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Binary items:** show the change in the *share answering Yes* (or the catalog's reference level) in **percentage points**. The subtitle would read "Change in share answering 'Yes', percentage points · 2023 → 2024".
2. **Ordinal items:** reuse Atlas's "Answer level" control and show the pp change in the share at that level. The transition table already covers the full picture.
3. If a mean-of-codes view is kept for 0–10 scales and counts, orient it using the catalog's `direction`. For items with `direction: none`, print the anchors on the axis ("← more often · less often →").
4. Apply the same orientation to the histogram's x-axis.

### C2 · Correlates rankings are headed by tiny-n items with r ≈ ±1 · **Critical · Bug**

**Location.** `/correlates`, ranked chart and "Across countries" matrix, correlation mode.

**Problem.** Ranking by |r| with no minimum n and no interval promotes items asked of a handful of people. Examples: Argentina #1 at n = 7; Germany #1 = −1.00 at n = 2; Japan #1 = +1.00 at n = 2. A two-point correlation is always ±1. Nothing in the chart signals n: it appears only in the tooltip and the collapsed table. The adjusted model drops these items, so the two modes disagree about the top result with no explanation.

**Why it matters.** The first thing a visitor sees on the newest page is a statistically meaningless headline, presented with the same visual weight as n = 6,700 results.

**Recommendation.**

- Exclude pairs below a minimum n from the *ranking*, for example n < 100 or an effective-n threshold. Keep them in the data table per ADR-0011, and add a footnote such as "3 items with fewer than 100 respondents are listed only in the data table".
- Alternatively, rank by the lower bound of a Fisher-z interval, which is cheap and would also give the dots CIs.
- Put n in the row, for example a muted "n = 7" after the label, or mark small-n rows with a hollow dot.

### C3 · Correlation signs follow code order, not label meaning · **Critical · Bug**

**Location.** `/correlates` (both models, ranked chart and matrix). The same root cause as C1.

**Problem.** The catalog already records `direction`, but predictors are correlated in raw code order:

| Label shown | Coding | Shown | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feeling down or depressed | 1 = Nearly every day … 4 = Not at all | +0.44 | *less* depressed ↔ more flourishing |
| Feeling nervous or anxious | same | +0.38 | *less* anxious ↔ more flourishing |
| Little interest or pleasure | same | +0.35 | *more* interest ↔ more flourishing |
| Feeling capable | 1 = Always … 4 = Never | −0.37 | *more* capable ↔ more flourishing |
| Life in balance | 1 = Always … 4 = Never | −0.38 | *more* balance ↔ more flourishing |
| TIPI: calm, emotionally stable | 1 = Agree strongly … 7 = Disagree strongly | −0.36 | *calmer* ↔ more flourishing |

"Feeling down or depressed +0.44" sits one row from "PHQ-2 depression score −0.44", so the list looks self-contradictory.

**Recommendation.** Orient every item so that a higher value means "more of what the label says" before correlating. Alternatively, rename labels to match coding, as the catalog already does for "Not feeling lonely", "Absence of suffering" and "Freedom from bodily pain". The first is better because it also fixes C1 and the Breakdowns and Compare means. Add a unit-test invariant: for every derived or reversed pair (PHQ-2 score vs. its items), the displayed signs must agree.

### C4 · Atlas "Distribution" of derived scores drops ~90% of respondents · **Critical · Bug**

**Location.** `/?outcome=sfi&stat=distribution`, with any countries selected. The same applies to the six SFI domain scores.

**Problem.** SFI is a mean of 12 items and therefore continuous, but the histogram bins only exact integers 0–10. For Germany the eleven bars sum to **~8.9%** and their n sum to **~850 of ~9,500**. The y-axis tops out at 10%, and the x-axis is titled "Answer (0–10)". The 16 September review saw the symptom ("tallest bin ~2.5%") but not the cause.

**Recommendation.** Bin continuous scores into intervals ([0,1), [1,2) … or 0.5-wide), label the axis "Score (0–10), binned", or hide "Distribution" for derived/continuous scores. Add a test that distribution shares per country sum to 100% ± rounding.

### C5 · Faceted grids lose their column labels · **Critical · Bug**

**Location.** `/compare` with "Split each country by", and `/breakdowns` with "Second breakdown".

**Problem.**

- Column (fx) labels render only **below the last row**. That is ~1,500 px down on Compare and ~5,200 px down on Breakdowns. For the whole scroll, the reader cannot tell which column is Brazil or which is "Secondary".
- On Breakdowns the labels are raw codebook value labels, around 100 characters each ("Secondary - Some secondary education, completed secondary education, or some post-secondary education (9-15 years of education)"), and they overlap into one unreadable line. The codebook typo "recieved" is visible.
- Facet titles repeat in every column and overlap their neighbours. Adjacent axes collide ("10.02.5").
- Tooltips give the row value but not the column ("5.74 SFI: close social relationships · 25–29", with no country).

**Recommendation.**

- Put fx labels at the top (`fx: { axis: "top" }`) and repeat them per row block, or make them sticky.
- Use short display labels for split levels, via a display-label override in the catalog ("Primary / Secondary / Tertiary").
- Add `fx` padding so adjacent tick labels cannot touch.
- Show the domain name once per row as a row header rather than in every cell.
- Include every facet value in the tooltip.

### H1 · Axis windows run past the measure's range · **High · Bug**

**Location.** What Matters country chart: **2–12** on a 0–10 scale (0–15 at 390 px). Breakdowns with a second breakdown: **0–15**.

**Problem.** The domain is "niced" from the data and CIs but never clamped to the scale's `min`/`max`, so the chart offers space for impossible values. With n = 2 cells (visible in Breakdowns tooltips) the CIs extend beyond 10.

**Recommendation.** Clamp every domain to `[catalog.min, catalog.max]` after niceing. ADR-0010's "fit the data" revision should fit *within* the scale. Clip CI whiskers at the bounds and mark clipped ends.

### H2 · Maps: the low end of the ramp merges with "no estimate" · **High · Bug**

**Location.** Atlas map; US States map (both themes).

**Problem.** In light mode the lowest bin is a near-white cream. Japan, the lowest-scoring country at 5.89, is almost invisible against unsurveyed land. The same happens to Nevada and Kansas. In dark mode the ramp inverts, so the lowest bins become near-black, like the background and the "no estimate" swatch. Legend end labels use mixed precision (5.89 / 8.1; 6.99 / 7.5).

**Recommendation.** Render "no estimate" and unsurveyed land as a hatch or a cool neutral grey with a visible outline. Start the sequential ramp at a clearly tinted step. Use matching precision on both legend ends.

For States specifically, the ramp spans 0.5 points while CIs are ±0.2–0.3. Consider a diverging scale centred on the US value, or state in the legend "range across states: 0.5 points on a 0–10 scale". Give pooled groups a pattern or outline so a pooled estimate is not read as Wyoming's own.

### H4 · Correlates matrix: hidden columns and page-level overflow · **High · Bug**

**Location.** `/correlates` → "Across countries".

**Problem.** The table is 2,084 px inside an 896 px container. macOS hides the scrollbar, so 14 countries are undiscoverable, and the last visible column is cut mid-word. At 390 px the *document* is 2,087 px wide and the whole page pans sideways. The likely source is the table `<caption>` (it measures the full table width) escaping the scroll container.

The tint is binned: +0.57 and +0.82 get the identical fill (`rgb(197,216,210)`), and the lightest bin is barely distinguishable from the paper colour.

**Recommendation.**

- Add a sticky first column, a right-edge fade, and a "→ 14 more countries" hint.
- Alternatively, transpose the matrix so countries are rows and the top ~10 measures are columns.
- Fix the overflow leak.
- Use 4–5 steps per sign with a stronger end colour, or a continuous scale.

### H5 · "Adjusted" changes units without changing the framing · **High · Design**

**Location.** `/correlates`, Model → Adjusted.

**Problem.** The chart title, layout and toggle stay the same, but values become SFI points per SD of the predictor and can exceed 1. The unit sentence is below the chart, and the control set is in the footnote.

**Recommendation.**

- Label the toggle "Correlation (r)" / "Adjusted difference".
- Put the unit in the subtitle: "SFI points per 1 SD of each measure, holding age, gender, education, employment and marital status fixed".
- Add an axis title.
- Don't reuse the −1…1 window.

### H9 · US States: the "US overall" line sits below nearly every state · **High · Bug (data consistency)**

**Location.** `/states`, chart view, Wave 1 SFI.

**Problem.** US overall is 7.11 (national weight `w_c1`). Of the 41 state and pool estimates (state weight `w_state_c1`), only VA, CT, KS and NV fall below it (37 are above). Every large state is above it. A population-weighted mean of the plotted estimates cannot sit at the bottom of their distribution, so the two weights evidently do not aggregate to the same total.

**Why it matters.** A reader either concludes "almost every state beats the national average" or that the chart is wrong.

**Recommendation.**

- Compute the reference on the state weight, as the weighted mean across the plotted states, so it is comparable.
- Or show both lines, labelled "US (state weights)" and "US (national weight)", with one sentence of explanation.
- Label the line at the top of the chart, not only below the axis.

### Other visualization issues

- **M7** Faceted dot plots (Compare, Breakdowns, What Matters) put the bottom axis on the last row's baseline, so tick labels collide with the last row's label and dot ("Japan 3", "80+ 4", "being a good person 2"). Give the facet axis its own margin.
- **M8** Dot plots with 1–3 rows are too short. Tick labels touch the first row, and the zero reference shrinks to a stub (`/change` with 1–2 countries; Atlas median with 3). Set a minimum plot height.
- **M9** CI lower bounds below zero print as "−0.0%" (`[-0.0%, 0.1%]` in Atlas distribution tables). Clamp the display at 0%, or choose a proportion-appropriate interval. That is a stats decision and deserves an ADR.
- **M10** Tooltips sit directly on neighbouring data. On Atlas, Philippines' tip covers Israel's dot. On Compare, it covers two countries. On Change, it covers the +0.50 tick. Tooltip text is ~10–11 px. Offset tips horizontally from the mark and raise the size to the 12–13 px body-small token.
- **L6** Bar charts draw the CI whisker in the bar colour, so the lower half of every interval is invisible inside the bar. Draw whiskers in `--ink` over the bar, or use a lighter bar fill.
- **L2** Axis ticks use ASCII "-0.50" while value labels use "−0.50". Tick precision changes with filtering (−1.00 → −0.2) on the same chart.

## D. UX / navigation review

### H6 · Change: dead controls and dead-end measures · **High · Bug**

The catalog has **no variable asked in both the midyear survey and an annual wave**: 15 variables are midyear-only and 90 are Wave 1 + Wave 2. So "2023 → mid-2024", "mid-2024 → 2024" and "2023 → mid-2024 → 2024" are disabled for every measure, permanently. The only explanation is a hover-only `title`, which is invisible on touch.

The Measure picker also offers 71 measures that cannot show change (53 Wave-1-only, 15 midyear-only, 3 with no waves), and each one leads to "Asked only once".

**Recommendation.** Remove the three midyear pairs until a measure supports them. Replace them with one line: "The midyear survey asked different questions, so change is measured 2023 → 2024." Then either filter the picker to Y1+Y2 measures or badge the others "asked once".

### H7 · What Matters: information architecture · **High · Design**

The first chart is 23 panels × 7 rows. The page's other three sections start ~5,000 px down, and "Two things that travel together" is a section whose content says it cannot exist yet. Per CLAUDE.md's phase rule, that should be an explicit "Not implemented" stub or omitted.

**Recommendation.** Lead with one compact view, such as a 7 × 23 dot matrix or heatmap with countries as columns and items sorted by overall importance, or a single panel with a country picker. Add an in-page table of contents. Drop the placeholder section. Define "By value" ("by average importance across the seven items").

### C6 · US States: server errors reported as "offline" · **Critical (pending re-verification) · Bug**

With the API healthy (`/health` 200, Wave 1 requests 200), every "2024" and "Midyear" request to `/v1/states` comes back without CORS headers. FastAPI does this on unhandled exceptions, so it is most likely a 500. The UI then shows "Live data service is offline — the standard views still work; filters and medians are unavailable", which is wrong on both counts.

First confirm against a freshly started `make api`, since a stale API process is possible, and check the server log. Independently of the cause, the fetch layer should distinguish "unreachable" from "request failed" and say, for example, "Couldn't load 2024 state estimates (server error)".

### Discoverability and defaults (M6 · Medium · Design)

- **Compare** opens empty. Preselect 3 countries, or offer a one-click "Try: Brazil, Germany, Japan".
- The country button's label changes meaning with state: "Choose up to 5 countries" → "4 of 5 countries" on Compare, and "All 23 countries" → "3 countries" on Atlas. The Atlas Distribution empty state refers to "the Countries control", but no control is labelled that. Use a stable label with the count: "Countries: all 23 ▾" / "Countries: 3 ▾".
- On **Change**, the prompt that unlocks the two best charts ("Pick up to four countries above…") sits *below* the chart. Move it next to the country button, or show the histogram for the top 3 by default.
- Correlates defaults to **Argentina**, the first country alphabetically. That is arbitrary for a first visit, and the n = 7 headline makes it worse.
- "Pearson / Spearman" and "Adjusted" appear with no inline explanation. Add a one-line hint or an info link to the Methods anchor.

### Consistency across pages (M3 · Medium · Bug/Design)

- **Header jumps.** Codebook uses a 1,024 px container with the nav inline beside the brand. Every other page uses 896 px with the nav on its own row.
- **Four page-title patterns.**
  - Visually hidden h2 plus a large serif lede: Atlas, Change, Compare, What Matters, Correlates, US States.
  - Small bold title with a heavy rule: Methods, Model cards.
  - Large serif title plus lede: Codebook.
  - No lede at all: Breakdowns.
- **`document.title`** is "Flourish Atlas" on every route, so tabs, history and bookmarks are indistinguishable.
- **Control labels.** Most are stacked above the control, but "Split each country by", "Second breakdown", "Country", "Split by" and "Question" are inline beside it. Several appear on the same page.

### Terminology (M2 · Medium · Bug)

The waves are called "2023 / Midyear / 2024" (Atlas), "mid-2024" (Change), "Midyear survey" / "the midyear survey of 2024" (What Matters), "Wave 1, 2023" (subtitles) and "Y1, MY, Y2" (Codebook columns and filters). The midyear survey ran November 2023 – December 2024, so "mid-2024" and "of 2024" are inaccurate.

"Family" (Codebook filter) vs "Topic" (everywhere else). "161 questions" (Codebook) vs "Search all 154 measures" (pickers), with no explanation of the difference. What Matters has "was asked in the Wave 1, 2023 and the Wave 2, 2024" (stray articles).

### Other interaction notes

- **M4** Nav items wrap onto two lines between roughly 650 and 790 px. At 768 px (iPad portrait) and a half-screen 735 px window, "What Matters" and "US States" wrap, and the active underline sits under a two-line label. This was also in the 16 Sept review as F17. Collapse into "More" at a higher breakpoint or `white-space: nowrap` the links.
- **L4 (uncertain)** A heavy focus outline stays on segmented options and buttons after a *mouse* click (Adjusted, "4 of 5 countries", "By value"). If the style keys off `:focus`/`:focus-within` rather than `:focus-visible`, switch it.
- The search dropdown shows raw variable codes (`BEAUTY`, `NATURE`) beside names. The Second-breakdown list appends codes to every option ("Abused when growing up (ABUSED)").
- The Model cards page is orphaned (not in the nav, no back link to Correlates).
- Invalid URL parameters produce a clear, dismissible notice. Back/forward restores state. The 404 page is good.

## E. Visual polish review

- **M5 · Native controls ignore the manual theme (Bug).** With the OS in dark mode and the site set to light, `color-scheme` stays `light dark`, so native checkboxes render dark. Unchecked boxes are solid dark squares, and checked ones show a light tick, which is inverted at a glance. This is visible in every country popover and on "Adjusted state weights". Fix: `:root[data-theme="light"] { color-scheme: light }` and the dark equivalent.
- **H8 · Dark-mode tooltips unreadable (Bug).** Every Plot tip keeps a white fill (`#FFF`) while its text switches to the dark theme's `#B6B1A4`, a contrast of about 2:1. Set the tip `fill` to the surface token and `stroke` to the rule token.
- **H11 · Phone clipping (Bug).** What Matters row labels are clipped at the left edge at 390 px. Codebook overflows the viewport by 66 px, and the "Chart it →" column is off-screen. Correlates overflows by ~1,700 px (H4).
- **M7 · Decorative colour.** Compare's six domain hues carry no meaning, orange and red-orange are close, and it is the only multi-hue categorical use on the site. Use the house ink, or one accent for the selected domain.
- **M15 · Heading hierarchy.** On What Matters, section headings ("How the ranking shifts within a country", "The other midyear questions") are smaller than the chart titles beneath them ("Japan by age band", "Arts and cultural activities").
- **Wrong or stale copy (Medium, Bug).**
  - Atlas → Median footnote: "Dots are point estimates — no confidence interval is computed for **a correlation**". This is Correlates copy, and it also appears on the map ("Dots" on a map).
  - US States chart footnote: "weighted so each **country's** sample stands for its adult population". The map footnote on the same page correctly says "state's".
  - Methods: "Until the population-rescaled 'all countries' option ships (Phase 5)…" and "when a number is withheld". Phase 5 has shipped, and ADR-0011 withholds nothing.
- **M1 · Internal identifiers in the UI.**
  - Weight codes in every tooltip (`w_c1`, `w_l2`, `w_l1m`, `w_state_c1`).
  - Pool keys (`ND_SD_WY`) and USPS codes as row labels.
  - "(needs the live service)" on Breakdowns.
  - "tints span ±1.42".
  - "Y1/MY/Y2".

  Most were already flagged as F6 on 16 September. Keep the weight code in the data table and Methods, and say "Wave 1 weight" in tooltips if anything.
